Gather these first and the whole thing takes about five minutes. If you cannot find something, guess. A rough plan you can see beats a perfect plan you never make.

1

A list of what you owe

Credit cards, store cards, car loans, student loans, personal loans, medical bills, buy now pay later, and money you borrowed from family. Leave your home loan out for now.

2

The balance on each one

This is the total amount still left to pay on that debt today, not the monthly payment.

3

The smallest payment allowed

Your statement will call this the minimum payment. It is the least you can pay each month without getting into trouble.

4

The interest rate

Often shown as APR and written as a percentage. Look on your statement or log in to your account. If you cannot find it, put in a rough guess for now.

How the debt snowball actually works

It is less about maths and more about keeping going. That is the whole secret.

Here is the idea in four steps. First, write down every debt you have, ordered from the smallest balance to the largest. Ignore interest rates for now. Second, pay the smallest allowed payment on every debt so nothing falls behind. Third, throw every spare bit of money you can find at the smallest debt until it is gone.

Fourth, and this is the part that makes it a snowball, once that smallest debt is paid off you take the whole payment you were making on it and add it to the next debt on the list.

That rolled over amount keeps growing. By the time you reach your biggest debt, you are hitting it with a payment far bigger than you could have managed at the start. Each debt you clear is a win you can see, and those wins are what keep people going long enough to actually finish.

Why not just pay the highest rate first?

You can, and that is called the avalanche method. On paper it saves you more money. But on paper assumes you never lose heart, never get discouraged by a huge balance that barely moves, and never give up. For a lot of people the early wins are worth more than the interest they would save. Use the toggle in the calculator to compare both with your own numbers.

Snowball or avalanche, which should you pick?

Two ways to order the same list. One is kinder to your motivation, the other is kinder to your wallet.

Snowball

Smallest balance first

Line the debts up by how much you owe, smallest at the top. Pay the least allowed on everything, and put your extra money on the smallest one.

  • You get your first win fast, sometimes in weeks
  • Best if you have tried before and given up
  • The wins keep you going
  • Costs a little more in interest
Avalanche

Highest rate first

Line the debts up by interest rate, highest at the top. Pay the least allowed on everything, and put your extra money on the most expensive one.

  • Saves the most money overall
  • Best if one card has a punishing rate
  • Your first win can take a while
  • Needs more patience to stick with

The honest answer is that the best method is the one you will actually finish. Run both above and see the real gap for your own debts. For most people it is smaller than the arguments online would suggest.

Money words, explained simply

If any word on this page made you pause, here is what it actually means. No one is born knowing these.

Balance

The total amount you still owe on a debt. If you paid it all off today, this is the number you would hand over.

Smallest payment (minimum payment)

The least you are allowed to pay each month to stay in good standing. Paying only this keeps you in debt for a very long time.

Interest rate (APR)

The fee a lender charges you for borrowing, shown as a percentage. A higher rate means more of your payment goes to them instead of your actual debt.

Interest

The actual money that rate costs you. It is the reason a balance can barely move even when you keep paying.

Debt free date

The month your last debt reaches zero, based on the numbers you put in and sticking to the plan.

Snowball

Paying off your smallest balance first, then rolling that whole payment onto the next debt so your payment grows each time.

Avalanche

Paying off your highest interest rate first. It saves more money if you can stay the course.

Principal

The original amount you borrowed, before any interest was added on top.

Questions people ask

The things worth knowing before you start.

Is this really free, and are my numbers private?
Yes to both. There is no signup, no payment, and no account. Everything you type is worked out inside your own browser on your own device. Your balances are never uploaded to us or anyone else. Close the tab and they are gone, so print your plan if you want to keep it.
Can I use this if I do not live in the United States?
Yes. Pick your currency at the top of the calculator and every number on the page updates to match. The method works exactly the same wherever you live, because the maths of debt does not change by country.
Which debts should I include?
Include credit cards, store cards, personal loans, car loans, student loans, buy now pay later balances, medical bills, and money you owe family. Leave out your home loan for now. Most people tackle that separately, after everything else is cleared.
What if I cannot afford anything extra right now?
Then put zero in the extra box. You will still see your date based on the smallest payments alone, and that is useful to know. When things change, even a small amount will pull that date closer, and you can come back and see exactly how much.
How accurate is the debt free date?
It is a good estimate, not a promise. To keep things simple it assumes your rates stay the same, you keep paying the same amount, and you add no new debt. Real life is messier than that, so treat it as a strong guide rather than an exact date.
Does paying off debt hurt my credit score?
Generally the opposite. Paying balances down usually helps over time because you are using less of your available credit. It often helps to keep accounts open as you clear them rather than closing them straight away.
Should I save money while paying off debt?
Many people keep a small cushion first, often around one month of essential costs, so that a surprise bill does not send them straight back to a credit card. Once that cushion exists, most spare money goes to the debt. What is right for you depends on your situation.
Why is my mortgage not included?
Home loans are usually much larger and much longer than other debts, and including one would swamp the plan and make your date look hopeless. Most people clear everything else first, then look at the house separately.
