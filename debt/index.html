How the debt snowball actually works

It is less about maths and more about keeping going long enough to finish. That is the whole secret.

List every debt from smallest balance to largest. Pay the smallest allowed on every debt so nothing falls behind. Throw every spare bit of money at the smallest one until it is gone. Then take that whole payment and add it on top of what you were paying on the next debt. That rolled-over payment keeps growing until you reach your last debt and clear it.

Snowball

Smallest balance first

Order by balance, smallest first. Pay minimums on all, extra on the smallest.

  • First win can come within weeks
  • Best if you have quit before
  • Keeps you going
  • Costs a little more interest
Avalanche

Highest rate first

Order by interest rate, highest first. Pay minimums on all, extra on the priciest.

  • Saves the most money overall
  • Best if one card has a punishing rate
  • First win can take longer
  • Needs more patience
Not sure which to pick? Go with the snowball. Most people do. The difference in interest is usually smaller than you think, and finishing matters more than optimising.

Money words, explained simply

No one is born knowing these. Here is what they actually mean.

Balance

The total amount you still owe on a debt today, not the monthly payment.

Minimum payment

The least you can pay each month without penalty. Paying only this keeps you in debt for a very long time.

Interest rate (APR)

The fee a lender charges you for borrowing, shown as a percentage. Higher means more of your payment goes to them.

Interest

The actual money that rate costs you each month. It is why a balance can barely move even when you keep paying.

Debt free date

The month your last debt reaches zero, based on your numbers and sticking to the plan.

Snowball

Paying smallest balance first, then rolling that payment forward so it grows each time you clear a debt.

Avalanche

Paying highest interest rate first. Saves more money overall if you can stay the course.

Principal

The original amount you borrowed before any interest was added on top.

Questions people ask

The things worth knowing before you dive in.

Got a question? Tap any one below to see the answer. And if it is not here, email us at info@clearpath.lifestyle and we will get back to you.
Is this free and are my numbers private?
Yes to both. No signup, no payment, no account. Everything you type is worked out inside your own browser on your own device. Your balances are never sent to us or anyone else. Close the tab and they are gone, so print your plan if you want to keep it.
Can I use this outside the United States?
Yes. Pick your currency at the top of the calculator and every number on the page updates to match. The method works exactly the same wherever you live.
Which debts should I include?
Include credit cards, store cards, personal loans, car loans, student loans, buy now pay later balances, medical bills, and money you owe family. Leave out your home loan for now. Most people tackle that separately after everything else is cleared.
What if I cannot afford anything extra right now?
Put zero in the extra box. You will still see your date based on the smallest payments alone. When things change, even a small amount will pull that date closer.
How accurate is the debt free date?
It is a good estimate, not a promise. To keep things simple it assumes your rates stay the same, you keep paying the same amount, and you add no new debt. Treat it as a strong guide rather than an exact date.
Should I save while paying off debt?
Many people keep a small cushion first, often around one month of essential costs, so a surprise bill does not send them straight back to a credit card. Once that cushion exists, most spare money goes to the debt.
Does paying off debt hurt my credit score?
Generally the opposite. Paying balances down usually helps over time because you are using less of your available credit. It often helps to keep accounts open as you clear them rather than closing them straight away.
Why is my mortgage not included?
Home loans are usually much larger and longer. Including one would make your date look hopeless and swamp the plan. Most people clear everything else first, then look at the house separately.
