Learn as you go

Personal finance, explained like you're a person

You should not need to know what APR means before you can make a smart decision about your money. So here.

APR

Annual Percentage Rate. The yearly cost of borrowing, as a percentage. Higher APR means more of your monthly payment goes to the lender instead of reducing your balance.

Monthly payment

The amount you pay each month. The minimum is the smallest amount to avoid penalty. Paying more than the minimum always reduces your balance faster.

Balance

The total amount you currently owe on a debt. Not what you originally borrowed - what is left after all your payments so far.

Debt-free date

The specific month your last debt reaches zero, based on your current payments and the strategy you choose. This is what Clearpath shows you.

Debt snowball

A payoff strategy where you attack the smallest balance first. Clears one debt quickly, builds momentum, and keeps most people motivated long enough to finish.

Principal

The original amount borrowed, before any interest. When you make a payment, part goes to interest first, then the rest reduces your principal.

Questions

Things worth knowing

Is Clearpath completely free?
Yes. The debt calculator, payoff planner, and all guides are free. No trial, no credit card, no account. Open a tool and start. The core tools will always be free.
Are my debt numbers private?
Completely. Everything you enter runs inside your browser. Nothing is sent to our servers, stored in a database, or accessible to anyone. Close the tab and the data is gone.
Which method should I use - snowball or avalanche?
If you have quit a debt plan before, choose snowball. The early win on the smallest debt keeps most people going. If you are highly disciplined and want to save the most money in interest, try avalanche. The free calculator lets you compare both with your real numbers.
Can I use this if my lender gives me a monthly rate?
Yes. The full tracker at clearpath.click/debt has a rate toggle that lets you switch between annual and monthly rates. If your rate is 2.5% per month, switch to Monthly and enter 2.5. The calculator handles the conversion correctly.
How accurate is the debt-free date?
It is a solid estimate based on your numbers. It assumes your rates stay the same and you keep paying the same amount. Treat it as a strong planning guide. Even a rough date is far more useful than no date at all - it gives you a target to work toward.
Can I use Clearpath for student loans, car loans, or medical debt?
Yes to all of them. You can include any type of non-mortgage debt. Leave your home loan out for now - most people handle that separately after clearing everything else.

Frequently asked questions

Is the guide really free?
Yes. You will not need a credit card or payment details to get it.
What if I have several debts?
That is exactly what the guide is designed to help you with. You will learn how to bring everything together and decide which debt to prioritise.
What is the difference between Debt Snowball and Debt Avalanche?
The Debt Snowball focuses on paying off your smallest balances first, while the Debt Avalanche prioritises debts with the highest interest rates. The guide explains both approaches so you can choose the one that fits you.
What if I do not earn very much?
You do not need a perfect income to start. The goal is to create a realistic plan around your actual financial situation.
Will this guarantee that I become debt-free?
No. We will not make unrealistic promises. The guide gives you a practical framework for understanding your debt and creating a plan to work toward becoming debt-free.
